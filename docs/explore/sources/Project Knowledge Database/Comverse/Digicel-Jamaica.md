# Digicel Jamaica – VAS Platform Relocation

**Company:** Comverse (later Mavenir)  
**Period:** June 2011 – February 2012  
**Role:** Senior Project Lead — Implementation Lead / Customer Delivery Lead  
**Industry:** Telecommunications  
**Country:** Jamaica / Haiti  
**Delivery Model:** Waterfall  
**Status:** Production Go-Live Completed

---

# Executive Summary

Following the devastating earthquake in Haiti, Digicel initiated the relocation of the Value Added Services (VAS) platform to Jamaica to ensure business continuity while continuing to support customers across both Jamaica and Haiti.

As the on-site implementation lead, I coordinated infrastructure installation, software upgrades, implementation activities, testing, customer communication, and production deployment while acting as the primary operational interface between Digicel and Comverse.

This project represented my first significant customer-facing leadership responsibility and became a defining experience in stakeholder communication, implementation governance, and proactive risk management.

---

# Business Context

Following the earthquake in Haiti, Digicel needed to relocate a critical VAS platform to Jamaica.

The objective was to restore and strengthen the operating environment while supporting traffic from both Jamaica and Haiti.

The project included:

- Reinstallation of relocated infrastructure.
- Validation of existing hardware.
- Replacement of missing or unusable components.
- Infrastructure expansion.
- Software upgrade.
- Integration with the mobile networks in Jamaica and Haiti.
- Testing and production readiness.

Approximately 60–70% of the original infrastructure was physically relocated from Haiti.

---

# Business Challenge

The project had to restore and modernize a critical telecommunications platform under significant operational constraints.

The environment required validation, reinstallation, hardware completion, software upgrades, and network integration before the solution could be released into production.

The implementation also had to support increased traffic requirements for both Jamaica and Haiti.

# Delivery Challenges

The project experienced several simultaneous challenges:

- Critical spare hardware was not available on-site.
- The initial schedule lacked sufficiently realistic milestones.
- Site access and security restrictions affected implementation activities.
- Technical resources on-site were limited.
- Infrastructure, software, and network dependencies created cascading delays.
- Replacement parts had to be sourced from other locations, including the United States and Israel.

The project was initially expected to take approximately six months and was ultimately completed in approximately nine months.

---

# Leadership Focus

The main leadership challenge was maintaining customer confidence while the implementation team worked through repeated operational constraints.

My focus included:

- Maintaining transparent communication.
- Tracking and escalating risks early.
- Managing customer expectations.
- Prioritizing implementation blockers.
- Coordinating customer and internal technical teams.
- Keeping the customer informed when delivery assumptions changed.

This project taught me that maintaining confidence during difficult delivery situations can be as important as resolving the technical problem itself.

# Leadership Reflection

This was one of my first projects with significant direct customer leadership responsibility.

Looking back, I learned that implementation risk needs to be identified early enough for corrective action to remain possible.

Dependencies between infrastructure, suppliers, technical teams, and customer readiness can become delivery blockers if they are not identified and actively managed.

I also learned that asking more experienced leaders for guidance early in a project can prevent avoidable mistakes.

This experience strongly influenced the way I later approached risk management, stakeholder communication, and project preparation.

---

# What I Would Do Differently Today

With the experience I have today, I would invest significantly more effort in creating delivery certainty before implementation started.

I would:

- Perform a complete inventory of the existing infrastructure.
- Validate the available environment against the target architecture.
- Confirm which hardware components could continue to be used.
- Identify missing hardware and other dependencies before execution.
- Validate the implementation schedule with both internal leadership and customer stakeholders.
- Confirm milestones and acceptance points.
- Establish a structured risk register with clear owners, mitigation actions, escalation criteria, and impact assessment.
- Identify potential blockers early enough to prevent hard stops in the delivery plan.

Today I understand that successful implementations depend less on solving technical problems quickly and more on reducing uncertainty before those problems become delivery risks.

---

# How This Project Changed the Way I Lead

- I learned to identify dependencies before they become blockers.
- I learned that delivery predictability starts before implementation begins.
- I learned that proactive customer communication is essential when plans change.
- I learned that project preparation strongly influences execution quality.
- I learned that asking experienced leaders for support early can improve delivery decisions.
