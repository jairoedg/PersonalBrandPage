The situation

7 sections

01

Overview

Claro Peru was executing a strategic telecommunications modernization initiative to introduce a new 4G mobile network.

To support this transformation, several existing services, including the Value Added Services platform, needed to be upgraded and integrated with the new network infrastructure.

As the on-site implementation lead, I coordinated the VAS upgrade, managed delivery dependencies, maintained customer communication, and supported integration, testing, and production readiness.

The project strengthened my experience in dependency management, commercial awareness, customer communication, and delivery coordination across multiple external and internal constraints.

02

Business Context

Claro Peru launched a strategic initiative to implement a new 4G mobile network.

As part of this initiative, several supporting telecommunications platforms needed to be upgraded and integrated with the new environment.

The Comverse VAS platform was one of those systems.

The project therefore had two main objectives:

  • Upgrade the existing VAS solution to a new software version.
  • Integrate the upgraded platform with the new 4G mobile network.
03

Business Challenge

The project depended on several conditions outside the immediate implementation team's control.

These included:

  • Hardware customs clearance.
  • Site and facility readiness.
  • Air-conditioning availability.
  • Infrastructure readiness.
  • Integration with Huawei's new 4G network.
  • Availability of software packages from Comverse.

The challenge was to keep the project moving while coordinating these dependencies and maintaining customer confidence.

04

Scope of Responsibility

I acted as the on-site implementation and customer delivery lead.

My responsibilities included:

  • Coordinating the VAS platform upgrade.
  • Tracking implementation progress.
  • Managing risks and issues.
  • Maintaining the project risk register.
  • Communicating project status.
  • Coordinating customer and internal teams.
  • Supporting integration planning.
  • Coordinating testing activities.
  • Supporting production readiness.
  • Escalating delivery blockers.

I was not responsible for overall project cost management or contract negotiation.

What I did

3 sections

01

Delivery Challenges

The project faced several delivery challenges.

External Dependencies

  • Hardware was delayed in customs for approximately three weeks.
  • Site readiness was affected by facility issues, including air conditioning.
  • Huawei's 4G rollout introduced additional integration dependencies.

Internal Dependencies

  • Software package delivery was delayed.
  • Reduced software readiness affected the available time for integration and testing.

These issues were tracked separately to maintain clear ownership and support objective schedule discussions.

02

Leadership Decisions

One of the most important aspects of the project was maintaining a clear view of schedule ownership.

Customer-related delays and internal delivery issues were tracked independently.

When external constraints delayed infrastructure readiness, the delivery team used that time to advance internal preparation and resolve software readiness issues where possible.

This reduced the impact of internal delays once the customer environment became available.

The objective was not to assign blame, but to maintain an evidence-based understanding of the project status and support fair schedule discussions with the customer.

This experience reinforced the value of negotiation based on facts, dependencies, and documented delivery history.

03

Delivery Approach

The project relied heavily on:

  • Continuous risk and issue tracking.
  • Weekly status reporting.
  • Transparent communication.
  • Dependency management.
  • Coordination between customer, Comverse, and third-party teams.
  • Replanning when external conditions changed.

The relationship with the customer remained collaborative despite schedule pressure.

What I learned

3 sections

01

Leadership Reflection

This project reinforced that delivery leadership extends beyond technical execution.

It requires understanding who owns each dependency, documenting delivery constraints objectively, and keeping communication constructive even when delays occur on both sides.

I learned that a collaborative relationship with the customer creates better opportunities for negotiation and recovery than an adversarial approach.

The experience also strengthened my understanding that external delays should not become idle time. They should be used to improve internal readiness whenever possible.

02

What I Would Do Differently Today

Today I would formalize dependency management much earlier.

In addition to a risk register, I would maintain a structured dependency register identifying:

  • External providers.
  • Infrastructure prerequisites.
  • Hardware availability.
  • Facility readiness.
  • Software delivery dependencies.
  • Network integration milestones.
  • Owners.
  • Expected dates.
  • Escalation points.

This would provide earlier visibility of schedule risk and make project discussions more objective.

03

How This Project Changed the Way I Lead

  • Effective negotiation starts with objective project information.
  • Customer-owned and supplier-owned issues should be tracked independently while maintaining a collaborative relationship.
  • External delays should be used to improve internal readiness where possible.
  • Strong customer relationships make difficult schedule discussions easier.
  • Dependency management can be as important as task management.
  • Clear ownership of risks and blockers improves delivery control.
