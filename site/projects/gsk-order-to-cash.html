GSK: Order-to-Cash for an Andean SAP Migration

TCS · Pharmaceutical · July 2017 – October 2018

Client
GSK
Role
e-Invoicing - Order to Cash (O2C) Stream Lead
Markets
Colombia, Peru, Ecuador, Chile
Program
JDE to SAP ERP Migration
Technology
SAP

GSK was executing a global ERP transformation program to migrate business operations from JD Edwards (JDE) to SAP.

The program was delivered in regional waves, with the Andean region covering Colombia, Peru, Ecuador, and Chile.

Within the wider transformation, I was responsible for leading the Order-to-Cash workstream, including the electronic invoicing integrations required in each country.

This was my first major project outside the telecommunications industry and my first significant exposure to SAP and pharmaceutical business processes.

The experience became an important step in my professional evolution because it required me to move outside my technical comfort zone, rapidly understand unfamiliar business processes, coordinate specialists across multiple teams, and translate between business, SAP, regulatory, and third-party provider concerns.

Outcome: Delivered for go-live.

Business Context

GSK initiated a global ERP modernization program to migrate from JD Edwards to SAP.

The transformation was organized into regional waves, with each wave covering a specific market or geographic area.

The Andean wave included Colombia, Peru, Ecuador, and Chile.

The project team combined:

  • GSK business employees with detailed country-process knowledge.
  • SAP consultants specialized by functional stream and based in London.
  • A local team in Bogotá responsible for building integrations with third-party providers.
  • External electronic invoicing providers.

A major delivery constraint was that GSK business stakeholders had to continue supporting normal operations in the existing JDE environment while also contributing time and expertise to the SAP transformation.

Their operational responsibilities remained the priority, which created recurring availability constraints for the project.

Business Problem

The Order-to-Cash process was a critical dependency within the broader ERP transformation.

The process covered activities from customer order creation through invoicing and payment completion.

Delays in the Order-to-Cash stream could affect the overall SAP program because the work depended on multiple upstream processes and external integrations.

Potential consequences included:

  • Delays to the broader program schedule.
  • Extended retention of external project resources.
  • Additional delivery costs.
  • Increased costs with third-party providers.
  • Delayed readiness across multiple countries.

The challenge was therefore not simply delivering an interface.

It was ensuring that a business-critical workstream remained synchronized with the broader transformation program.

Order-to-Cash workstream for the Andean region:

  • Colombia

My Responsibilities

  • Lead delivery of the Order-to-Cash stream.
  • Understand the end-to-end Order-to-Cash business process.

  • Coordinate SAP consultants and business stakeholders.
  • Lead electronic invoicing interface delivery.

Order-to-Cash Scope

The stream included the business processes required to support the end-to-end sales lifecycle.

A key component was electronic invoicing, including documents such as:

  • Invoices
  • Credit notes
  • Debit notes

The solution also depended on upstream processes and other functional streams, including sales and distribution and warehouse/distribution activities.

Because Order-to-Cash was positioned toward the later stages of the wider business process, delays or changes in upstream streams could directly affect delivery of the final invoicing solution.

Delivery Challenges

Business Stakeholder Availability

GSK employees responsible for country processes still had to operate the existing JDE business environment.

Their project responsibilities competed with business-as-usual responsibilities.

The challenge was not lack of engagement, but limited availability.

Cross-Functional Dependencies

The Order-to-Cash process depended on several other business and technical streams.

Changes in upstream processes could affect:

  • Order creation.
  • Distribution activities.
  • Invoice generation.
  • Electronic invoicing interfaces.

This required continuous coordination across teams.

Regulatory Change

Electronic invoicing requirements changed during project execution.

Colombia

In 2017, Colombia introduced new electronic invoicing requirements.

This required:

  • Selection of a new provider.
  • Definition of a new process.
  • Evaluation of the most suitable solution for GSK.
  • Integration of the selected provider into the SAP migration plan.

Ecuador and Peru

Additional regulatory changes occurred during delivery in both Ecuador and Peru.

These changes affected:

  • Initial design decisions.
  • Budgets.
  • Delivery timelines.
  • Third-party provider agreements.

The project therefore had to absorb regulatory changes without negatively affecting the overall SAP go-live.

Leadership Decisions

1. Learn the Business Before Trying to Lead the Solution

Because SAP and pharmaceutical business processes were new to me, my first priority was to understand the Order-to-Cash process and the electronic invoicing landscape.

I needed enough understanding to connect discussions across business teams, SAP consultants, technical teams, and external providers.

This became essential for making meaningful delivery decisions.

2. Establish Clear Communication Across Multiple Teams

The workstream involved several groups with different perspectives:

  • SAP consultants defining the target SAP process.
  • Business stakeholders explaining how the existing country processes worked.
  • Local integration teams developing interfaces.
  • Third-party electronic invoicing providers.
  • Program management teams in Colombia and the UK.

I created a clear communication path so that requirements, issues, dependencies, and changes could be understood across all groups.

3. Translate Between Business and Technology

A major part of my role was translating concerns across teams.

This included:

  • Explaining business requirements to technical teams.
  • Helping business stakeholders understand SAP constraints.
  • Coordinating changes with electronic invoicing providers.
  • Connecting regulatory changes to schedule and budget implications.

This ability to connect different perspectives became one of the most important capabilities I developed during the project.

4. Manage Dependencies Early

Because Order-to-Cash was affected by earlier business-process stages, dependency management became critical.

I had to continuously understand how decisions in other streams could affect the final invoicing solution.

This reinforced the importance of identifying cross-stream dependencies early rather than treating each workstream independently.

Results

The Order-to-Cash solution was delivered in time for the planned SAP go-live.

The workstream did not introduce delays to the broader program despite:

  • Regulatory changes in Ecuador and Peru.
  • Introduction of a new electronic invoicing model in Colombia.
  • Coordination across four countries.
  • Dependencies on multiple business and technical streams.

Third-party provider contracts were managed within the available budget and formally closed.

The project was delivered without negatively affecting the overall transformation schedule.

Leadership Reflection

This project was my first major experience outside my established technical comfort zone.

I entered the engagement with limited knowledge of SAP, pharmaceutical operations, and the Order-to-Cash process.

The first stage was difficult because I needed to understand a completely new business and technology environment while already being responsible for delivery.

The experience taught me that a delivery leader does not need to begin as the deepest subject-matter expert.

What matters is developing enough understanding to ask the right questions, connect the right people, translate between different perspectives, and help specialists move toward a common objective.

Building strong relationships with the people who had the expertise I lacked became essential to succeeding in the role.

The recognition I received from GSK reinforced another lesson: persistence, continuous follow-up, and supporting people through difficult situations can materially influence delivery outcomes.

How This Project Changed the Way I Lead

  • When entering an unfamiliar domain, my first priority is to understand the business process before trying to control the delivery.
  • I do not need to be the deepest technical expert, but I need enough understanding to connect the people who are.
  • Complex delivery problems are often caused by dependencies between teams rather than problems inside a single team.
  • Translation between business, technical, regulatory, and supplier perspectives is a core leadership responsibility.
  • Asking for help and building strong relationships with subject-matter experts accelerates learning and improves decisions.
  • Business continuity and transformation work must be planned together because operational responsibilities will continue competing for stakeholder attention.
