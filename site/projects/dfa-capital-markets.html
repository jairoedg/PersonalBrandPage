The situation

7 sections

01

Overview

DFA relied on Charles River Development's Investment Management System (IMS) to support several core investment-management processes, including equity trading, portfolio management, and fixed income.

When Charles River migrated the relevant platform capabilities to Azure, DFA needed to modernize the way its locally hosted business processes connected to IMS.

Endava was engaged to provide a multidisciplinary engineering team to build an API Gateway, refactor approximately 60 business processes, and support the transition to the new cloud-based integration model.

My role included participating in team setup, project governance, delivery documentation, and ongoing Agile delivery leadership.

The engagement became one of the most important projects in my professional development because it strengthened my ability to lead a technically complex Agile delivery while maintaining close alignment with the client, technical leadership, business stakeholders, and Endava delivery management.

02

Business Context

DFA used Charles River Development's Investment Management System as part of several business-critical investment-management processes.

Relevant areas included:

  • Equity trading.
  • Equity portfolio management.
  • Fixed income.
  • Other investment-management workflows supported by IMS.

Charles River migrated its platform to Azure.

As a result, DFA needed to transition from locally connected processes toward a cloud-based integration model.

The transformation required the existing DFA processes to continue operating with minimal business-logic disruption while adopting the new IMS connectivity model.

03

Business Problem

DFA did not have sufficient internal engineering capacity and technical expertise to complete the required integration and refactoring work at the required pace.

Endava was engaged to provide:

  • C# developers.
  • Data Engineers.
  • Automation Testers.
  • Performance Testers.

The technical objective was to create an API Gateway that would operate as an intermediary between local DFA processes and the cloud-hosted IMS platform. It also included refactoring and migrating data stored in databases that were no longer connected to the new IMS solution.

After establishing that integration layer, approximately 60 existing business processes needed to be refactored so they could communicate with the new IMS version while minimizing changes to the underlying business logic.

What I did

Delivery Challenges

01

gRPC Architecture Complexity

DFA's architecture team selected gRPC as part of the API Gateway implementation.

This created a significant learning curve for the delivery team because the approach was more complex than the REST API patterns with which the team was more familiar.

The complexity affected early delivery expectations and contributed to schedule pressure.

The issue was resolved by bringing in an experienced developer who could understand the architectural intent and translate it into practical guidance for the wider team.

02

Business-Process Knowledge

The project required refactoring approximately 60 business processes.

The engineering team depended heavily on business stakeholders to understand the existing behavior and identify process-specific requirements.

Limited stakeholder availability created repeated delivery blockers.

These issues had to be escalated several times so that business participation could be improved.

03

Team Stability

Team health and continuity were critical.

Changes in team composition had a significant delivery impact because new team members typically required approximately four weeks to acquire sufficient business and technical knowledge.

This was particularly challenging because the engagement operated under a continuous release cadence aligned with the wider program.

04

Continuous Delivery Pressure

The project was not delivering a single final release.

The team needed to progressively refactor and release business processes while maintaining alignment with the broader program roadmap.

This increased the importance of:

  • Prioritization.
  • Release planning.
  • Dependency management.
  • Stakeholder coordination.
  • Team knowledge retention.

Leadership Decisions

01

Treat Risks as Triggers for Delivery Improvement

I used identified risks as signals that the delivery model might need to change.

Rather than only tracking issues, the objective was to adapt the way the team worked whenever the original assumptions were no longer effective.

02

Bring in the Right Expertise

When the team struggled with gRPC, the response was not to continue pushing the same approach.

An experienced developer was brought in to bridge the gap between the architectural definition and practical implementation.

This accelerated the team's understanding and helped stabilize delivery.

03

Escalate Missing Business Support

The engineering team could not successfully refactor business processes without sufficient knowledge from DFA stakeholders.

When business participation became a blocker, I escalated the issue and worked with the client to improve availability and coordination.

04

Keep Decision-Makers Closely Aligned

Project reviews were scheduled every two weeks and included key technical and delivery decision-makers.

These reviews helped maintain alignment across:

  • Technical leads.
  • Client Project Management.
  • Sponsors.
  • Endava delivery leadership.

The objective was to reduce delays in decisions and keep roadmap changes visible.

05

Work Collaboratively With the Client

A major strength of the engagement was the close working relationship with DFA.

The client was generally supportive of changes required to improve delivery.

This made it possible to address issues with less bureaucracy and focus on solving problems collaboratively.

What I learned

4 sections

01

Leadership Reflection

This project gave me the opportunity to apply Agile delivery practices in a much more mature and practical way than in my previous experiences.

I learned that the value of Scrum or Kanban does not come from following ceremonies mechanically.

The value comes from using the principles behind them to improve transparency, decision-making, team alignment, and delivery flow.

The project also reinforced the importance of adapting the delivery approach when initial assumptions no longer apply.

When technical complexity, stakeholder availability, or team changes affected delivery, the right response was not to protect the original plan at all costs.

It was to make the problem visible, involve the right decision-makers, agree on the required change, and keep the team aligned with the product vision and program milestones.

The strong working relationship with the client made this possible.

02

What I Would Do Differently Today

Today I would formalize several areas earlier in the engagement:

  • Business-process ownership for each refactored workflow.
  • Knowledge-transfer expectations when team members change.
  • Technical onboarding for complex frameworks such as gRPC.
  • Explicit dependency mapping between business, technical, and release activities.
  • Capacity buffers for team transitions.
  • Clear escalation thresholds for missing business support.

I would also establish stronger mechanisms for retaining business and technical knowledge so that team changes had less impact on delivery continuity.

03

How This Project Changed the Way I Lead

  • I learned how to apply Agile principles pragmatically rather than mechanically.
  • I became more confident adapting roadmaps and delivery plans when evidence showed that the original assumptions were no longer valid.
  • I learned to use regular stakeholder reviews to accelerate decisions and keep technical and business leaders aligned.
  • I became more deliberate about protecting team health and continuity.
  • I learned how critical business participation is when technology teams are refactoring complex domain processes.
  • I strengthened my ability to communicate risks and issues clearly to both client and internal stakeholders.
  • I became more comfortable leading delivery in a highly regulated and technically complex Capital Markets environment.
  • I gained more experience supporting presales, contract discussions, and new opportunity development.
04

Professional Evolution

This engagement marked a significant step in my development as an Agile Project Manager.

Earlier in my career, I had learned delivery primarily through Waterfall projects and later through a difficult first experience with Agile.

This project gave me the opportunity to apply Agile delivery successfully in a complex real-world environment.

I learned how to combine:

  • Agile practices.
  • Project-management discipline.
  • Technical understanding.
  • Stakeholder management.
  • Client partnership.
  • Delivery adaptation.

It also strengthened my confidence in leading projects where technology, business processes, and delivery governance are tightly interconnected.
